# Role review: Deaf ASL user "Jordan" (simulated, 2026-11-15)

> **Simulated perspective.** This is an AI role-play of a fictional Deaf ASL instructor and advocate, grounded in published Deaf-led commentary. It **does not replace review by actual Deaf signers**, who should be paid and credited.
>
> Limits: no AI-specific NAD statement was found, and most of the error tips come from general teaching sites.

You ruled out translation and made-up signs, and the checker admits what it can't see. Good. Now the hard part.

## 1. Focus
- **Fingerspelling is the wrong front door.** Deaf-led researchers criticize tech that treats fingerspelling as the language.
- **Parents need other things first:** everyday signs (MILK, MORE, FINISH, MOM), eye contact, ways to get the child's attention, and a Deaf mentor (ASDC SignOn).
- **Fingerspelling comes in around week 3:** the child's name, family names, the doctor, the street.
- **Room mode is backwards.** TABLE, DOOR and LAMP have signs, so drilling T-A-B-L-E teaches spelling words that Deaf people sign.

## 2. Errors hearing learners make
- **Producing:**
  - they bounce on every letter;
  - they say letter names instead of mouthing the word;
  - they rush, then stall;
  - their hand drifts toward the face;
  - they mush M/N/T, E and K/V;
  - they get double letters wrong.
- **Reading:** they decode letter by letter and lose the word. Fluent readers see the shape of the whole word.
- **A hold-and-check loop builds that choppiness**, and letters-per-minute plus streaks reward speed over clarity.
- **What to do instead:**
  - The wrist is already tracked, so flag bouncing, and grade rhythm, transitions and whole words.
  - If the tutor hand only blends between textbook poses, learners will learn to read a robot. Record it from several real Deaf fingerspellers.

## 3. Ghost hand
- It helps with handshapes in week one, but it is not a breakthrough. Mirroring is a small, temporary hurdle.
- The real skill is reading other people's hands from the front while watching their face.
- First person also hides how your hand looks to the person watching.
- **Add a "reader's view", fade the ghost over time, and support left-handed spellers.**

## 4. Before going public
- **Pay a Deaf ASL teacher** to check every letter, the tutor's movement and the text. Credit them by name, with their consent. "If at all possible" is not enough.
- **Call it "ASL fingerspelling practice", not "learning ASL".**
  - Say it covers ASL only: some letters, such as E, have accepted variants, and other countries use different manual alphabets.
- **State the limits:** it can't see faces, mouthing or gaze.
- **Don't use deaf kids as a sad hook.** Language deprivation is real (NAD), but this app doesn't solve it. Point to Deaf-led resources.
- No Deaf stories or faces without consent, payment and credit.
- **Say what it is:** a tool for hearing learners, not access for Deaf people.

## 5. The two proposed additions
- **(a) Multiplayer between learners.** Two beginners correcting each other lock in their errors, so limit hints to what the checker says.
  - A Deaf partner works only if they are paid and set the terms.
  - The headset hides your face from them.
- **(b) Translation helper: no.**
  - A tracker that can't grade M or N cannot read real signing. Grammar lives in the face and in space.
  - WFD/WASLI warned against avatars replacing human signers, and efsli says AI "is often pitched as a replacement".
  - Hospitals would say "we have the app" and skip the interpreter.
  - Deaf people would be pushed to sign slowly and largely "for the machine".
  - Reading strangers' hands without consent is surveillance.
  - It would betray the honesty the pitch is built on.

## 6. Would Jordan recommend it?
**Not yet.** Maybe as a supplement for families who already have a Deaf mentor. To earn a recommendation it needs:
- a credited Deaf reviewer;
- an honest scope;
- names and places as the practice words;
- real Deaf spellers in reading mode;
- links to Deaf teachers.

## Top 5 changes
1. Pay and credit a Deaf reviewer **before any public footage**.
2. Re-scope all wording to "fingerspelling practice, a supplement".
3. Grade movement, not frozen poses: bounce, transitions, whole words.
4. Use names and brands as practice words, and record the tutor hand from Deaf fingerspellers.
5. Add a reader's view and fade the ghost. Never add a translation helper.

## Review checklist for the built app
1. All 26 letters, including the J and Z movements, are signed off by a named, paid Deaf signer.
2. "Learn ASL", "translate", "fluent" and "interpreter" appear nowhere: not in the app, the video or the Devpost entry.
3. The app states what it can't do: signs, grammar, faces.
4. Left-handed mode grades correctly.
5. Deliberately bouncy spelling never gets a perfect score.
6. Double letters are taught and checked.
7. The reading-mode hand comes from two or more Deaf spellers and looks natural at every speed, confirmed by a Deaf reviewer.
8. Users can see per-letter grading reliability and which letters are never graded.
9. Room mode never drills a word that has a common sign without saying "this has a sign".
10. The first session and the end screen link to Deaf-led learning (Deaf teachers, ASDC SignOn, local Deaf events).
11. Nothing reads anyone else's hands, and nothing records.
12. The pitch uses no deaf children or Deaf stories without consent, payment and credit, and never implies the app fixes language deprivation.
